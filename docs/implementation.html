What the program has to staff and decide

The student-facing site says what a student does. This page says what has to be true on our side for that to happen: eight challenges for faculty, what we propose for each, and who owns it — then, for the chair, the dean's office, and MILO, what we are asking for and what it returns.

Lemma (Locus of control). Start with the smallest piece that is yours to change this semester. Propose the rest only with a semester of evidence.

Each item below is marked Proposed (we can start without a vote) or Open decision (needs the group, the chair, or MILO). Nothing is marked Decided yet.

1

Mentor load

The model depends on faculty replying to every student, every semester. That is the first thing to collapse under load; the samples make it look easier than it is.

Open decision

What we'll do

  • Cohort review day. One afternoon per semester. Each mentor reads their students' newest artifact page and leaves one comment. Two scheduled events a year replace an open-ended reply obligation.
  • Cap of 12 students per mentor. Above that, add a mentor, not students.
  • Between review days, feedback comes from the course instructor who assigned the artifact and from a peer-read pairing set up in Methods I.
  • Counts toward service load, in writing, or it will not survive year two.

Owner

Program coordinator · department chair

Needs a call

Open decision: does the review day count as service? Needs the chair.

2

Reflection that is actually reflection

Without taught, cued, artifact-linked reflection, the working group's warning applies: it is a website or a LinkedIn page. Students default to summary. All three samples are strong students.

Proposed

What we'll do

  • Two-criterion rubric, nothing else scored. (1) Is the claim tied to a specific place in the artifact? (2) Did the student reply to feedback? Length and eloquence do not count.
  • A before / after pair on the site. One summary-style reflection, the mentor comment that pushed it, and the revision — on the artifact-template page and in Methods I.
  • Reflection is assigned, not requested. Every course that produces an artifact assigns the reflection with it, using the Process / Outcomes / Integration / Development prompts.

Owner

Methods I & II instructors

3

Minimum vs. full

MILE is a graduation requirement, which makes the portfolio a checklist. Some students will do only the minimum. The site never says whether that is acceptable.

Open decision

What we'll do

  • The MILE minimum is the program minimum. About Me, three ASC artifacts, Advanced Writing, Capstone, concluding reflection.
  • Everything else is required by the course that assigns it, not by the portfolio. Letters, philosophy versions, and the feedback log are Methods assignments with Methods grades.
  • Consequence: no student "fails the portfolio." They miss a course assignment, which already has a grade attached.

Owner

Program faculty

Needs a call

Open decision: confirm the minimum-is-the-minimum rule, or set a higher program bar.

4

No baseline for current students

Nothing can be revisited if Year 1 was never captured. The first cohort of juniors and seniors has no baseline. The Late Start sample is really the model for everyone in the first two years.

Proposed

What we'll do

  • Fall 2026 is a build-backward semester for every current major. One assignment in each math ed course this fall: a then / now letter and one retrospective artifact with a retrospective reflection.
  • Reframe the Late Start template as the launch plan, not the exception. Marcus's site is what every current junior's site should look like by December.
  • From fall 2027, UNV 101 shells carry the Year 1 baseline as designed.

Owner

All math ed instructors, fall 2026

5

ASC designations are not settled

The artifact map assumes specific math and EDT courses are Applied Skills Courses. MILO's first call is limited to a few submissions per department.

Open decision

What we'll do

  • Ask MILO now which math and EDT courses will be designated in the first call; propose MTH 231 (quantitative reasoning) and EDT 313 (oral communication or teamwork).
  • Until answered, the site says "a course designated ASC" rather than naming one.
  • Fallback: if no math ed course is designated, ASC artifacts come from wherever the student takes them and the math ed artifacts stand alone. The portfolio still works; it just doesn't double-count.

Owner

Program coordinator · MILO

Needs a call

Open: waiting on MILO's designation call.

6

Video and privacy

District consent, anonymized student work, and share-don't-publish are covered as a FAQ answer. Not covered: who audits it, and what happens when a district says no video.

Proposed

What we'll do

  • One-page protocol, owned by the placement coordinator: consent form on file before filming; video stored in a shared Drive folder, never on the site; the site embeds a link only.
  • Substitute rule, written now: if a district refuses video, the Capstone artifact is a lesson plan plus student-work pair with the supervisor's observation notes.
  • Share, don't publish. Students add instructors as viewers and remove them after grades post — the Week 3 recommendation, restated in the syllabus.

Owner

Placement coordinator

7

Program assessment

The site is built entirely around the student's learning. The program will need to answer whether portfolios get sampled for program review and accreditation, and how permissions work.

Open decision

What we'll do

  • Sample, don't census. The Capstone instructor pulls a random 20% each spring; two faculty score on the two-criterion rubric; results go to program review.
  • Students are told in Year 1 that this happens.
  • Permissions: MILO's entity account is already tied to every shell, so the sample can be read without students re-sharing.

Owner

Capstone instructor · assessment lead

Needs a call

Open decision: start with the first cohort (spring 2027) or wait one cycle.

8

Sustainability past the pilot

Most of these efforts end when the champion moves on. The working group's locus-of-control framing exists for this reason.

Proposed

What we'll do

  • Run the Course Pilot this semester in one Methods section. Google Doc with tabs, two artifact cycles, feedback log.
  • Put the end-of-semester debrief on this page — what worked, what to change — before proposing the four-year version to the department.
  • Two owners from the start. A second faculty member co-owns the mentor review day so it isn't one person's project.

Owner

Pilot instructor + one co-owner

For administrators

What we are asking for, and what it returns

Five asks, each small on its own. Together they are the difference between a portfolio program that runs for a decade and one that runs for the length of one faculty member's enthusiasm. Costs are honest estimates; the returns are what the department and college get back.

AskWhy it mattersCostWhat it returns
Service credit for the mentor review dayChair

Two afternoons a year per mentor, reading ~12 students' newest artifact page and leaving one comment each. If it isn't service, it's volunteer work, and volunteer work stops.

~8 hours per mentor per year, recognized in the annual service record.

The feedback loop that makes this a learning portfolio rather than a file cabinet. Also: mentors read real student work across the program twice a year, which is program review by another name.

MILE requires three Applied Skills Course artifacts. If math ed courses are designated, the math ed artifacts satisfy MILE instead of adding to it. If not, students carry both.

Two course proposals in MILO's first call. Instruction on quantitative reasoning and oral communication is already in these courses; the proposal documents it.

Zero extra artifacts for math ed students, and a department presence in the first ASC cohort.

Consent forms, Drive storage, district refusals, the substitute rule. Someone has to own it or it becomes each student's problem the week before Capstone.

~1 hour per week during placement semesters; a one-page protocol written once.

Licensure-ready classroom video for every graduate, collected under a defensible privacy process — and a hiring artifact principals actually ask for.

Two faculty scoring ~6 portfolios on a two-criterion rubric each spring: an afternoon.

Direct evidence of planning, instruction, and assessment for CAEP / Ohio program review, drawn from work students already did — instead of a separate assessment artifact nobody wanted to make.

A named second ownerChair

The working group's locus-of-control framing exists because most portfolio efforts end when the champion leaves. Two owners from the first semester, not the third.

One faculty member co-owning the review day and the fall build-backward assignment. No release time requested.

A program that survives a sabbatical.

For program review

A random sample of student portfolios, scored on a shared rubric, is the cleanest outcomes evidence the department can produce — and MILO's entity account already has read access to every shell, so the sample costs no student re-sharing.

For recruitment and placement

Every graduate leaves with a shareable site containing a taught lesson, a student-work analysis, and classroom video with commentary. That is the interview. Principals in the Talawanda and Cincinnati-area districts we place in have said so directly.

For the college

ePortfolios are an AAC&U high-impact practice with the research behind it — 6–10% higher pass rates, +5 points retention in the Eynon studies. A department-level implementation that satisfies MILE is exactly the model MILO is looking for from its first ASC call.
